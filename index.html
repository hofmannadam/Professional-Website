Adam Hofmann

MD, CM, FRCPC · General internist · CEO, Impelix Health Inc.

I am a Royal College–certified general internist trained at McGill University, where I served as chief resident in general internal medicine, and a diplomate of the American Board of Internal Medicine. I have practiced hospital-based internal medicine continuously since 2010 — currently in Saint-Jérôme, Quebec, and as a locum internist in Ontario — and have taught with the Université de Montréal Faculty of Medicine since 2009. I hold active licenses in Ontario, Quebec, and New York, and I am relocating to Toronto in January 2027. My work sits at the intersection of clinical medicine, technology, and the question of how health systems can serve the people they too often fail to reach.

Current work

I am the CEO of Impelix Health Inc., a medical device company developing a wearable bioimpedance system for monitoring peripheral edema in heart failure, cirrhosis, and kidney disease — conditions in which fluid status drives hospitalizations, and in which earlier, quantified detection can change outcomes. A first-in-human study is planned at CISSS des Laurentides (Saint-Jérôme) for 2026–27, building on my 2025 systematic review of bioimpedance for edema assessment.

Clinically, I am staff internist at Hôpital régional de Saint-Jérôme (CISSS des Laurentides), where I have practiced since 2012 — CCU attending, ward and emergency consultation, and a full-scope general internal medicine clinic that, in a regional hospital without on-site endocrinology or rheumatology, spans the community scope of those specialties. Since 2026 I have also served as a locum internist in Ontario (ICU, emergency consultation, ward).

Teaching & service

I have been a clinical teacher with the Université de Montréal Faculty of Medicine since 2009, regularly supervising senior general internal medicine residents, and was named Teacher of the Year at Hôpital du Sacré-Cœur de Montréal (2010–2011). I served as an elected board member of the Québec Medical Association (2018–2020) and as resident member of the board of directors of the SMBD Jewish General Hospital (2006–2009). As a medical student I founded the Asclepius Project, a humanistic-medicine society recognized with a Forces Avenir award (no longer active), and trained as a clown-doctor with the Gesundheit Institute, including fieldwork alongside Patch Adams. My teaching interests centre on clinical communication — including a medical-improv curriculum on reading the room in patient encounters — and on how physicians can build, rather than merely inhabit, the systems they work in.
